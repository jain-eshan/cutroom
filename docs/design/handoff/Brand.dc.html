3bTwo names in the reference's register
Your reference uses verb-plus-ly: a plain action word made into a product. It's friendlier than 1a Cutaway and it scans as software, not as film-school vocabulary. Two in that shape, so you have the option:
Framely

Says the one thing the tool does — it frames people — in the register a beginner already trusts. Slightly generic, and "-ly" is a crowded shelf.

Cutly

Shortest, most app-like, easiest to say out loud in a video. Loses the precision of Cutaway — "cut" alone suggests trimming, which is the one thing this tool doesn't do.

I'd still take 1a. The reference earned its name with 28k stars behind it; on day one a name that explains the product does more work than one that sounds like a startup.
3cThe uncomfortable one
Desktop packaging isn't feature #15. It's feature #0.
Your reference ships a binary. Its install story is a download button, an AUR package, and a Homebrew-style formula. Ours is: install uv, install ffmpeg, get a Hugging Face token, accept a model licence, run two terminal windows.
No amount of branding closes that gap. 2d makes the failure diagnosable, which is the right interim move — but every design decision in these two files is capped by it, and the people you named as the audience (new creators, small editors) are exactly the ones who won't get through it.
If you only take one thing from the reference: it's not the visual style, it's that 28k people could install it without a terminal.
1 Naming & brand directions Pick one name and one direction; the product design in the next file inherits both.
What I read

README, UX_PRD.md, FEATURES.md. The pipeline is Upload → Processing → Cast → Editor → Export, all local, 15 features of which 9 ship today.

Your PRD deliberately deferred colour, type, spacing and motion to a separate design pass. This is that pass.

Positioning I'm designing to

Not "AI video editor". The honest claim is narrower and better: it does the camera work you don't have a camera operator for.

One camera in, a cut episode out. Everything decided in advance, everything overridable. That framing survives contact with a beginner; "AI" doesn't.

Two risks driving the design

Setup before value. A person who can't get the service running never sees the product. Treated as a real screen, not a README line.

The five-minute wait. Filled with the actual work appearing — transcript, faces, names — so it reads as progress, not a hang.

The name — five candidates
1aRecommended
Cutaway

The editing term for cutting away from the wide shot to something closer. It is the product: the tool's whole job is choosing when to cut away to whoever is talking.

Real word · reads as craft, not AI · teaches the concept it names

1bMost literal
Two-Shot

Cinematography for a frame holding two people — the exact shape of every podcast recording you accept as input. Says "we know how this is framed."

Hyphen is friction in a URL · breaks if you ever support 3+ hosts

1cWarmest
Roomtone

The ambient sound of the room, recorded so edits can breathe. A signal to audio people that this was built by someone who respects the craft.

Beautiful but opaque · says nothing about video or editing to a beginner

1dMost product-shaped
Turnstile

Your atomic unit is the speaker turn. A turnstile is the thing you pass through, one at a time — which is literally the editing motion.

Collides hard with a well-known Cloudflare product · I'd rule it out

1eMost ownable
Lapel

The clip-on mic. A small, humble, physical object that every podcaster owns. Short, unclaimed, memorable, and completely non-hypey.

Zero descriptive payload · needs a tagline doing all the work
